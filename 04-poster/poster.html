Exit Check
Know the cost of leaving

You will find out what leaving costs on the worst possible day.

The day the price changes. Exit Check answers the question in advance, keeps a copy outside every platform you depend on, opens that copy to prove it is readable, and reports what did not come out.

The entire output of the product

Leaving Notion today would take about 34 hours and no direct spend, and you would leave behind databases, database relations, comments, sharing and permissions and page history.

154 h of work to be running elsewhere, across one ordinary six service portfolio
390,834 items that would not come with you, of the 573,461 you hold
0 of those six platforms publish what leaving would cost

Exit cost, measured

Notion 64
Evernote 54
Slack, free 53
Figma 46
Google Photos 28
GitHub 26

Six weighted factors, each read from a field you can inspect. No model produces this number, which is why you are allowed to disagree with it. One rule overrides the arithmetic: if more than a quarter of what you hold cannot be exported at all, the service is trapped whatever the score says.

Four steps

Run the platform’s own export

Every day, week or month, without anyone remembering to.

Write the copy where you control it

Exit Check never holds the only copy of anything.

Open it, and say what is missing

A download that never gets opened is not a backup. Files are parsed, not counted.

Answer in one sentence

Hours, money, and exactly what stays behind.

What it deliberately is not

Not a model, because a number you cannot argue with is a number you cannot trust. Not a migration tool, because moving your data is a different product with a different failure mode. Not another place your data lives. And not a dashboard to visit: it should stay silent until a number moves, then send one sentence.

No model. No migration. No new place your data lives.
